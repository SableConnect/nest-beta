The automatic safety check looks only for words that may mean a child is at risk, and our safety team reads only messages reported to it. We process this information with the parent's consent, or where the law allows it, such as to protect a child.

Children and parents

A family is set up by a parent or legal guardian aged 18 or older, who consents in the app for themselves and their children. Children can't sign up on their own. Each parent chooses a monitoring level for each child, and the child is told which one applies:

  • Full view (suggested under 13): parents can read the child's chats.
  • Safety view (suggested 13 to 15): parents see who the child chats with and when, and the text of messages flagged for safety.
  • Activity only (suggested 16 and 17): parents see who the child chats with and when, but not what they say.

At every level, parents can see photos, hear voice notes and see call times in their children's chats. A friend from another family can chat with a child only after both families' parents approve. A child can also talk to Nest's safety team privately, without their parents being told, so that they always have somewhere to turn. We don't tell their parents unless the law requires it.

When a child chats with a friend from another family, the friend's parents can also see the chat according to their own settings. Family members such as grandparents can chat with everyone in the family, and parents can read those chats. Parents from different families can chat only if both agree to connect.

Security

Information is encrypted while it travels between the app and our servers and while it's stored. The database itself enforces who can read what, and when our technical staff look up messages directly in the database, it's logged.

Beta notice: messages are not yet end-to-end encrypted, so they're stored on our servers in a form our systems can read.

If someone gains access to personal information without permission, we'll tell the Information Regulator and the people affected as soon as reasonably possible (POPIA section 22).

How long we keep it

  • We keep information while the family account is active. A parent can delete the account in the app at any time (Settings, then Delete account); if no other parent in the family has joined Nest, the whole family is deleted straight away. How to delete a Nest account.
  • Deleted messages are removed straight away, and deleted photo and voice files the same night, unless they're held for a report to Nest.
  • Reports to Nest are kept for 12 months after they're closed, or until any referral to the authorities ends.
  • Beta sign-ups are kept until 12 months after the beta ends, unless you ask us to remove them sooner.
  • Chats brought over from WhatsApp are kept until the person who brought them over deletes them or leaves Nest, or the Nest chat is deleted.
  • Records of our safety team's work (who opened which report, and actions taken) are kept for 24 months.
  • Copies in our providers' backups are deleted automatically, normally within 30 days. Sign-in records (email address, internet address and time) may be kept longer, for security.

Your rights

You can see the main details Nest holds about you on the My profile screen in the app. You can ask us at any time to show you what we hold about you or your children, correct it, delete it, or stop processing it, and you can withdraw your consent. A parent can also do this in the app by removing a child or deleting the account. Email privacy@sableconnect.app; we'll respond within 30 days.

We send Nest news only to people who ticked the box when signing up. To stop it at any time, email privacy@sableconnect.app and we'll stop straight away.

Giving us information is voluntary, but Nest can't work without a parent's email, the children's first names and birth years, and the messages themselves.
